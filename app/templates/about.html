Project Overview

About DAR

A transparent, open machine learning system built to protect Indian job seekers from recruitment fraud.

What DAR is

DAR is an open decision-support tool created to help Indian job seekers evaluate employment opportunities before applying or paying any money. Every year, thousands of students and recent graduates fall victim to fraudulent job offers, training fees, and cyber scams.

DAR combines transparent Natural Language Processing (scikit-learn with TF-IDF features) with public scam awareness and community verification to identify red flags before harm is done.

How DAR Works (Architecture & Pipeline)

1

Input

The job seeker provides job information via plain text, pasted URL, or a screenshot/flyer from mobile camera or device storage.

2

Read Text

Direct text is parsed immediately; images are processed in-memory via Tesseract OCR and instantly discarded; web URLs are scraped safely.

3

Clean Text

Shared cleaning routine strips HTML tags, lowercases characters, normalizes phone numbers and URLs, and removes noisy punctuation.

4

TF-IDF Features

Extracts unigram and bigram TF-IDF word scores, weighting vocabulary that distinguishes legitimate company postings from fraudulent lures.

5

Model Scoring

Evaluated by Logistic Regression (default) or Random Forest using tuned probability cutoffs (0.62 for LR) to account for data imbalance.

6

Explanation

Produces an interpretable confidence gauge, highlights high-impact words in the posting, and lists concrete red flags found in the text.

7

Community Verification

Registered community members cast thumbs up (genuine) or thumbs down (fake) votes. The collective verdict is shown alongside the AI prediction to provide mutual corroboration.

System Limitations

In accordance with scientific and engineering honesty, DAR documents its real-world limitations in plain words:

  1. Class Imbalance: Public research data contains very few fake examples (866 in the 17,880 raw dataset), making fake job detection more challenging than the raw accuracy number suggests.
  2. Domain Shift: The models learned from structured job-portal text; social media posters, chat flyers, and WhatsApp forwards look different, which can reduce real-world accuracy.
  3. OCR Noise: OCR mistakes can alter words extracted from compressed images; users are encouraged to inspect and edit extracted text before analysis.
  4. Language Support: DAR currently evaluates English postings only. Postings in Hindi or regional Indian languages are not yet supported.
  5. Dataset Errors: Ground-truth labels in public datasets may occasionally contain misclassifications or historical noise.
  6. Decision Support Only: The model outputs a statistical probability, not legal proof. It should never substitute for verifying a company directly on its official website.
  7. Ad Matching Thresholds: Same-ad matching relies on cosine similarity of text embeddings; near-duplicate variations may occasionally miss or mis-link.
  8. Cold Starts: Free cloud hosting may spin down during idle periods and require up to 60 seconds to wake up for initial visitors.
  9. Community Voting Independence: Community votes may reflect individual opinions or biases; they are intentionally presented separately from the algorithmic AI verdict.

The Name "DAR"

"Dar" (डर) translates to fear in Hindi. The name reflects the mission of the system: fraudulent recruiters and deceptive job rackets should fear transparent, data-driven detection.
